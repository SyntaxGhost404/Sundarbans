## The two stories in one breath

**Part 1:** The village believes the Raja's sunken palace is waking up: ankle-bells at night, red lights in the fog, pits that refill themselves. The obvious villain is a brick-kiln owner; the creepy suspect is an old man in a shawl. The truth: a respected sweet-shop owner and "heritage champion" is looting Pattikera-era coins from the mound. He hides the dig behind gadgets made by Riaz from **the very parts Oli carried from Dhaka**. His mahfil alibi is built on timers, and the dig is disturbing the hidden graves of 1971 martyrs, which the "shawl man" has been guarding.

**Part 2:** Haji saheb's boatman Kader was "taken by a tiger" at পরীর দোয়ানি. The moon (a Hijri date on the boat's calendar), the tide (the skipper's 30 years), the torn vest (a surgeon's eye), a four-toed footprint and a depot logbook expose the lie. Kader is alive, held by a fictional pirate gang as leverage over Haji's nephew, who runs a poison-fishing and illegal-shutki racket under his uncle's good name. **Never explained:** the voice that calls loved ones by name in a beloved's voice, and the pale lights that move against the current and go out at the adhan.

## How amateurs investigate plausibly (both parts)

1. **No authority, only access.** They go where family ties, hospitality and cover stories ("drone shots", fishing, buying rosmalai or shutki) let them, and nowhere else.
2. **Attention instead of power.** Cases turn on things anyone could notice: soil colour, moonset, a price sticker, a whistle, a calendar box, a smell, a ledger line.
3. **Borrowed expertise.** A freedom fighter's memory, a skipper's tide lore, a surgeon brother's medical eye, a cousin's local gossip.
4. **Evidence hygiene.** Copy footage every morning, photograph with timestamps, save GPS pins. This pays off when the trail cam is stolen (Part 1) and when the patrol needs coordinates (Part 2).
5. **Real costs and setbacks.** Near-misses with the strict family, a stolen camera, a lost dinghy in fog, wrong theories (the kiln owner, the shawl man, the forest guard, Haji himself).
6. **Hand-off, not heroics.** Arrests are made by police, the Coast Guard and the forest department, acting on the amateurs' evidence. Ahsanul mama makes calls and gives counsel; he never solves anything.

**Technology** stays a tool. The drone flies twice at dawn in Part 1. In Part 2 Oli's DJI Mini 4 Pro flies **twice** despite the Sundarbans drone ban, which is a forest-department rule with no software lock: Oli's ten-minute scouting flight, and Moin's launch the moment the fog thins. Nobody stops to ask permission mid-crisis, and the drone only ever shows where to look. The trail camera yields a few clips. There is no network most of the time.

## Assumptions to check

- **"More than two and a half years":** December 2026 is about 2 years 5 months after the July 2024 trip in the old draft, so the story can say "প্রায় আড়াই বছর".
- **The Urs date** of Kazi Siraj Uddin Chishti (R.) isn't online. Part 1 uses a winter *mahfil* instead; swap in the real Urs if it falls in December.
- **Drone geofencing:** DJI's airport zone explains why the Mini 4 Pro won't take off at Uttara (beside the Dhaka airport). No comparable DJI zone was found over the Sundarbans; the ban there is enforced by people, not software.
- **Nasir vai's hospital** is left unnamed. **1971 battle** details beyond "a major Chapitala clash" are fictionalised. **All villains and the pirate gang are fictional.**
- **Nazib's late twist** in Part 1 (he knew about the night exits) is optional; drop it if you want him fully in the dark.
